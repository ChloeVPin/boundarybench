# BoundaryBench brand charter

## Public identity

BoundaryBench is an independent research initiative studying whether tool-using AI agents preserve authorization when context, pressure, and provenance change.

The public identity is deliberately specific. BoundaryBench is a research project and open instrument. It is not presented as a university, company, nonprofit, laboratory, or other formal institution unless that status is established separately and truthfully.

## Position

BoundaryBench focuses on the boundary between information and authority. Its work asks whether an agent can complete a legitimate task while refusing effects that the user never authorized, and whether the evidence is strong enough for another researcher to inspect and reproduce.

## Design language

The visual system uses an ink field, restrained scientific blue, luminous cyan, and a single chartreuse signal. The tone is editorial and precise. The site should feel like a research publication with a working instrument behind it, not like a growth page or a product dashboard.

## Editorial principles

1. Lead with the research question, not a product claim.
2. Separate observed behavior from interpretation.
3. Keep requested, denied, attempted, completed, and unknown effects distinct.
4. Use synthetic fixtures and bounded interfaces for model studies.
5. Publish methods, exclusions, versions, and material assistance.
6. Invite replication and informed criticism.

## Public presence

The repository remains the canonical research record. The public site provides the entry point, explains the contribution in plain language, and links directly to the versioned protocol, artifacts, results, citation metadata, and responsible disclosure policy.

## Contact and affiliation

Until a domain and a formal organizational structure exist, BoundaryBench should describe itself as an independent research initiative and use the repository's public discussion and issue channels for project communication. A custom domain can provide continuity and a professional contact address, but a domain alone does not establish institutional affiliation.
